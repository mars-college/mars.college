# Mars College — Frequently Asked Questions

## What is Mars College?

Mars College is an annual pop-up, off-grid college town in the California desert.
Every winter since 2020, its community builds a temporary campus on a dry lake bed
by the Salton Sea near Bombay Beach, California — assembled mostly by hand from
pallet racks and plywood, powered by solar, with water, internet, and other services
worked out together from first principles. For three months it runs like a full
college town: classes, art, events, a co-op gallery, a film festival. Each spring it
is torn down and the desert is left as it was found.

## Is it a real college?

It behaves like one — semester rhythm, classes, midterms, a final exhibition — but
there is no tuition, no grades, no degrees, and no faculty/student divide. Everyone
teaches and everyone learns. Mars calls itself an "uncollege."

## Where is it?

On a ~20-acre off-grid parcel of former lakebed near Bombay Beach, on the Salton Sea
in Imperial County, Southern California — about three hours from Los Angeles and San
Diego.

## When does it run?

Build: November–December. The season: January 11 to late March. Mars Electronica, the
final exhibition festival: March 26–28, 2027. Unbuild: March 29 through May 1.

## How much does it cost?

About $1,500 total for three months on the ground: roughly $500 in Treasury fees
(toilets, internet, shared water, aid), about $900 in camp dues (mostly food), and
$100–$600 for a place to sleep if you don't have a van. There is no tuition. DPW
build crew attend free (housing, meals, fee waived), and grant recipients and
micro-business operators earn most or all of it back.

## How do I join?

Apply at https://mars.college — admissions are rolling, and earlier is better.
Joining starts with applying to one of the camps: DPW (the build crew,
https://dpw.mars.college), the Department of Emergent Civilization (the makerspace, formerly known as Tool Camp), MIT (the Mars Institute of
Technology, the AI school), Co Lab (community practice), or MMA (the Martian Music
Academy, https://music.mars.college) — or by proposing a new camp
(info@mars.college).

Each camp runs its own application, so start from the camp page you want. MIT's
form is on https://mars.college/mit; Co Lab applies by email to
hello@communitylab.camp.

## What do I have to do while I'm there?

Contribute in a real way: cook, teach, build, repair, run a concession, offer a
service, or take a role in DPW, Treasury, or a camp. Each person is asked to leave
something behind — a presentation, a performance, an installation, a class, a
ritual, a service.

## Can I keep my remote job?

Yes. Enabling the dream life of remote workers is part of Mars College's reason for
being. The wifi is fast, the calendar is open, and remote workers make up a good
share of every cohort.

## Do I need construction experience for the build?

No. Most builders arrive without it. The work is real — pallet-rack assembly,
plywood walls, simple wiring, basic plumbing — and you work alongside people who
have done it for five or more seasons.

## Where do people sleep?

Most people live in vans, campers, or RVs. Those without one can rent or build a
housing pod through DPW ($100–$600 for the season), or arrange housing within a
camp. There is no hotel; you live on site.

## What is the weather like?

Winter in the low desert: warm sunny days, cold nights (sometimes below freezing),
low humidity, occasional wind and dust. December is mild; the season avoids the
brutal desert summer.

## What do people study?

The calendar is open — anyone can teach. Past terms have included creative AI, vibe
coding, filmmaking, music theory, ceramics, breadmaking, fermentation, creative
writing, screenwriting, yoga, bodywork, contact improv, geometry, philosophy, solar
power, vanlife skills, 3D printing, digital architecture, foraging, and desert
ecology. The formal AI program is the Mars Institute of Technology
(https://mars.college/mit).

## Who runs Mars College?

The community runs itself. It was co-founded in 2020 by Gene Kogan and Freeman and
now operates as a do-ocracy: a federation of camps with volunteer circles, an
elected council, and a transparent shared treasury. There are no employees.

## Why do you do this?

In Mars's own words: "Because we love the scene Mars cultivates and we think more
people should live like this. Mars is an experiment in what a small, self-organized,
deeply strange community can build together. We want to keep making it, and we want
to share it with the people who'd be at home here."
