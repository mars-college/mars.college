# About Mars College

Mars College is a seasonal, off-grid, AI-native art and technology community in the
Southern California desert. Every winter, its members build a temporary campus — a
small town made of pallet racks, plywood, solar panels, and vans — on a ~20-acre
parcel of open desert near Bombay Beach, on the shore of the Salton Sea. They live
there together for about three months, studying AI, making art, running a village,
and practicing a low-cost, high-tech, nature-oriented lifestyle. In spring the campus
is taken down and the desert is returned to bare ground. The community persists
year-round online and through its houses, projects, and diaspora, then returns the
next winter to build again.

Mars College calls itself a college because it behaves like one: it has departments,
classes, a semester rhythm, midterms, a final exhibition, and a campus. But there is
no tuition, no grades, no accreditation, and no faculty/student divide — everyone
teaches, everyone builds, everyone contributes. It is best understood as a
self-organized educational community and a recurring experiment in collective
off-grid living, with creative AI as its academic spine.

## Quick facts

- **Founded:** 2020 (first season ran as "Brahman"; renamed Mars College in August 2020)
- **Founders:** Gene Kogan (artist/programmer, creative-AI pioneer) and Freeman
  (community builder; previously founded Jaaga in Bangalore, Mars's direct precursor)
- **Location:** off-grid desert campus near Bombay Beach, Salton Sea, Imperial County,
  California
- **Season:** build in November–December; program from early January to late March;
  unbuild through April
- **Scale:** on the order of a hundred people living off-grid at peak season,
  organized into semi-autonomous camps, plus a larger year-round online community
- **Cost:** no tuition. A season on the ground costs about $1,500 total (shared
  services + food + housing); build-crew members attend free and many participants
  earn costs back through micro-businesses and grants
- **Website:** https://mars.college
- **Next season:** Mars 2027 (Season 8) — applications are open and rolling. See
  https://mars.college/mars-2027.md

## What makes it distinctive

1. **The city is the curriculum.** Building and operating an off-grid village —
   power, water, kitchens, internet, governance — is not logistics that supports the
   school; it *is* the school, alongside the AI program.
2. **AI-native since day one.** The founding 2020 season already ran a 16-node
   Raspberry Pi AI cluster and trained language models on its own chat logs. Every
   season since has had AI at its core: GPT-3 house bots (2022), Creative AI as the
   declared major (2023), an AI film festival (yearly since 2023), and autonomous
   agents helping run camp infrastructure (2026). The community's flagship
   generative-AI platform is Eden (https://eden.art), and its AI school is the Mars
   Institute of Technology (https://mars.college/mit).
3. **Self-governing.** Mars runs on a "do-ocracy" — decisions are made by the people
   doing the work — structured through camps, circles, an elected council, and a
   transparent shared treasury. There is no staff and no central authority.
4. **Radically cheap, deliberately comfortable.** Mars exists to prove that a
   high-tech creative life can be lived on a few hundred dollars a month, powered by
   sunshine, without sacrificing fast internet, hot showers, good coffee, or serious
   compute.
5. **Temporary by design.** Like the desert festivals it is adjacent to, Mars leaves
   no permanent buildings. Unlike a festival, it lasts a whole season and behaves
   like a functioning town, then archives itself and dissolves.

## The name

"Mars" is partly a joke about Mars colonization — the campus sits on cracked,
sun-baked lakebed that looks convincingly Martian — and partly serious: the community
treats the desert as a frontier for prototyping how small groups might live
self-sufficiently with renewable energy and AI. Members call themselves "Martians."
Campus landmarks carry science-fiction names: the central hall Chiba and its bar
Chatsubo are named for William Gibson's *Neuromancer*; nearby community houses are
named Saturn and Venus.
