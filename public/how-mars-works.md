# How Mars College Works

Mars College is not a company, a nonprofit with staff, or a festival with ticketing.
It is a self-organized community that rebuilds itself every year. This page explains
its operating system as of the 2027 season.

## The annual cycle

- **Off-season (spring–fall):** the campus does not exist. The community plans the
  next season online, publishes announcements on Substack, and runs online programs.
- **Build (November–December):** the DPW crew and early arrivals raise the campus
  from bare desert — pallet-rack structures, plywood walls, solar power, water
  systems, kitchens, internet.
- **Season (January–late March):** the full community lives on campus. Classes,
  projects, talks, exhibitions, and village operations run daily.
- **Unbuild (April):** everything comes down. Materials are inventoried and stored;
  the desert is returned to bare ground by May 1.

## Camps

Mars is a federation of **camps** — groups of people who commit to live together as
a cluster for the season, each with its own intake, footprint, dues, and character.
Recent and current camps include the DPW build crew (https://dpw.mars.college),
the Department of Emergent Civilization (the makerspace, formerly known as Tool
Camp — https://dec.mars.college), the Mars Institute of Technology (the AI
school, formerly AI Camp — https://mars.college/mit), Co Lab (community and
governance practice — https://colab.mars.college), and others that form season
to season. Camps internalize some services (usually food) and pool the rest
through the Treasury.

## DPW — Department of Public Works

**DPW** (https://dpw.mars.college) is the build organization, run by co-founder
Freeman. It rents structural materials to camps and individuals — pallet racks,
plywood, solar equipment, water tanks, camp gear — and builds and maintains the
village's civic infrastructure. DPW crew members work about six hours a day during
build and unbuild in exchange for free attendance: housing for the season, waived
fees, and a meal plan. DPW also licenses **concessions**: member-run
micro-businesses that sell services like fridge rental, purified water, solar
service, food co-op membership, mail, housing pods, transport, and vehicle repair.

## The Treasury

Shared village services — toilets, internet, drinking water, trash, shared spaces,
mutual aid — are funded by a transparent community **Treasury**, collected from
everyone on the ground and publicly accounted. For 2027 the Treasury fee is about
$500 per person for the season.

## Governance

Mars runs as a **do-ocracy**: decisions are made by the people who do the work.
Structure exists where it is needed — camp leads coordinate across camps, circles
handle cross-cutting domains (admissions, treasury, governance, calendar), and since
2025 an elected **Council** holds community-wide questions. There are no employees
and no central authority; contribution is the core value.

## What it costs (2027)

- **Treasury fee:** ~$500 (toilets, internet, water, shared spaces, aid, grants)
- **Camp dues:** ~$900 (mostly food, plus camp infrastructure)
- **Living space:** $100–$600 if you arrive without a van or camper (rent a housing
  pod or build)
- **Total:** roughly $1,500 for a three-month season — no tuition, ever.
- **Earning it back:** DPW crew attend free; members can run micro-businesses
  (building, water, solar, food, transport, repair) paid by the Treasury, camps, or
  individuals; and grants fund village gifts — bakeries, galleries, kilns, tool
  libraries, large-scale art.

## What is expected of participants

Contribute in a real way. Cook, teach, build, repair, run a concession, offer a
service, or take a role in DPW, Treasury, or a camp. Each Martian is asked to leave
something behind: a presentation, a performance, an installation, a class, a ritual,
a service. Remote workers are welcome and common — the campus has fast internet, and
the open calendar makes it practical to keep a full-time job while living on Mars.

## Community norms

- Members are known by their handles — the same name on Discord and in person.
- Consent is a practiced ritual, especially around using anyone's image, voice, or
  likeness in AI work (opt-in, with explicit permission tiers).
- Emotional support runs through dedicated, opt-in care structures.
- The community works in public: its history is documented in newsletter posts, films,
  zines, and an extensive archive.

## Location and environment

The campus sits on a ~20-acre off-grid parcel of former lakebed near **Bombay
Beach, California**, on the Salton Sea in Imperial County, with the town of Niland
and the off-grid community Slab City nearby. Winter days are warm and sunny, nights
can approach freezing, and everything runs on solar power. Mars maintains a
year-round footprint in Bombay Beach through community houses, including Saturn (the
social hub) and Venus (a workshop and storage compound).
