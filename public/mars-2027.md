# Mars College 2027 — Season 8

Mars College's eighth season runs from a November 2026 build through an April 2027
unbuild, with the residency proper from January 11 to late March 2027 in the desert
near Bombay Beach, California. **Applications are open now and rolling.** Apply via
https://mars.college (email info@mars.college with questions).

## Four ways in

1. **DPW — build the village.** Join the build crew: raise the campus from bare lake
   bed from November through spring, run its physical systems, and attend free
   (housing, waived fees, meal plan; ~6 hrs/day during build and unbuild).
   https://dpw.mars.college
2. **Department of Emergent Civilization (DEC, formerly known as Tool Camp) — the makerspace.** A project-based residency in Mars's shared
   workshop: saws, drills, 3D printers, kilns, tools, and desks for ~20 people
   building off-grid, DIY, deployable infrastructure. Cost $1,000–$1,500.
   https://dec.mars.college
3. **MIT — study AI.** The Mars Institute of Technology: classes, art, a gallery,
   the AI film festival, and off-grid AI engineering — how to engage the digital
   world while sustaining an off-grid life. Includes a fall online cohort.
   https://mars.college/mit
4. **Co Lab — practice community.** A three-month immersion in coherent community
   for 8–12 camp members: sociocratic governance, relational practice, and daily
   ritual. https://colab.mars.college

## Costs

About $1,500 for the three-month season: ~$500 Treasury fee (toilets, internet,
water, shared spaces, aid) + ~$900 camp dues (mostly food) + $100–$600 for a living
space if you don't bring a van or camper. There is no tuition. DPW crew, grant
recipients, and micro-business operators earn most or all of it back.

## Grants and micro-businesses

Grants (open from July 2026, deadline December 1) fund village gifts: bakeries,
galleries, kilns, tool libraries, large-scale art. Micro-businesses — building,
water, solar, food, transport, repair — are paid by the Treasury, camps, or
individual Martians. Returning services include a Brazilian café, a bakery, a
library, solar shade, and a bodywork dojo.

## Who should apply

Artists, engineers, builders, researchers, remote workers, and nomads who want to
spend a winter living cheaply and well in an off-grid village, studying AI on their
own terms, and contributing something real to a self-organized community. No
construction experience is needed for the build; remote workers can keep their jobs
(the wifi is genuinely fast). Each Martian is asked to leave something behind: a
class, a performance, an installation, a service, a ritual.
