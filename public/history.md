# History of Mars College (2019–2026)

Mars College has run seven consecutive winter seasons in the desert near Bombay
Beach, California, growing from a ~20-person experimental residency into a
self-governing federation of camps. The 2027 season will be its eighth. This is the
arc, season by season.

## 2019–2020 — Brahman, the founding season

The community began as **Brahman**, a winter art-and-technology residency co-founded
by **Gene Kogan** (artist and programmer, an early figure in creative AI) and
**Freeman** (community builder, whose earlier Jaaga project in Bangalore pioneered
the pallet-rack architecture Mars still uses). The first season opened in early 2020
on an off-grid pallet-rack pad east of Bombay Beach, with solar power and a
sixteen-node Raspberry Pi AI cluster. From the start the project was AI-native: the
first cohort ran generative-art installations and trained a language model on its
own chat logs. COVID cut the season short, but the community held together online.
In August 2020 it renamed itself **Mars College**, launched mars.college, and opened
applications for the next cohort. "We are all Martians" became the identity that
survived the founding season.

## 2021 — the residency takes shape

Roughly forty people spent January–March 2021 living and working in Bombay Beach
under careful COVID protocols. The season established the durable rhythm: build
weeks, workshops, communal kitchens, project showcases, and a shared calendar. Solar
arrays went up on the desert campus, the Mars Library opened, and the cohort's
signature artifact — a tarot deck generated with early text-to-image models
(CLIP-guided networks and StyleGAN) — captured what Mars was becoming: a place where
new AI tools are absorbed within weeks of appearing and turned into collective art.

## 2022 — tribes, house bots, and the arcology

The 2022 campus organized the central pallet-rack megastructure, **Chiba**, into
four elemental quadrants, with the cohort divided into matching Earth, Water, Air,
and Fire tribes. Each tribe built a GPT-3-powered Discord bot embodying its house
spirit — the bots could talk, run the lights, and banter with each other — under the
season's manifesto that "the desert village should demonstrate sentience, agency,
awareness, intelligence, creativity, and consciousness." The community also began
articulating itself publicly, and **Eden** (https://eden.art), the generative-AI
platform built by Mars-affiliated engineers, became the community's creative engine.

## 2023 — Creative AI becomes the declared major

Mars 2023 was the first season externally recruited on its AI theme. Daily
simulation sessions, machine-learning lectures streamed from the Saturn house, art
made with custom-trained diffusion models, and a wave of member-built bots and
AI apps made creative AI the organizing academic principle. The season coincided
with the GPT-4 moment, and the cohort lived inside the cascade — adopting new models
and tools the week they appeared. The season also produced Mars's first formal AI
consent practices: opt-in norms for training models on members' faces and voices,
worked out in real time by the community.

## 2024 — infrastructure season

Mars 2024 built the most serious physical plant yet: a community solar micro-grid
(the "Pleb Power Company") that generated over two megawatt-hours across the season,
a unified central structure, and the densest academic schedule to date — six
teaching departments spanning Creative AI, music, physical art, lifestyle, and a
culinary institute, plus weekly Thunder Talks and an AI film festival. The **Mars
Treasury** was formalized: a transparent, crowdfunded pool that pays for shared
services (toilets, internet, water), fully accounted and closed out each season.

## 2025 — self-governance

Mars 2025 was the inflection point where the community became explicitly
self-governing. Semi-autonomous camps federated around a central commons;
a Camp Leads circle, Admissions circle, and Governance circle formed; and the season
ended with the community electing its first **Council**. The residency format
reorganized around Ideas Week, Declaration Days, open-house weekends, and a final
exhibition. As co-founder Gene Kogan put it that January: "this is just a movement
now, and it's going to happen whether or not I have anything to say about it."

## 2026 — agents, an academic semester, and the Guardian

Mars 2026 ran with an elected council, a storytelling-centered curriculum, and the
community's first in-house AI agents helping operate camp infrastructure — including
a kitchen serving over twenty people with agent assistance. The season culminated in
**Mars Electronica**, the end-of-season exhibition and awards, and a full Martian
takeover of the Bombay Beach Biennale's tenth-anniversary "Year X." In March 2026,
The Guardian published a feature on Mars College — "No tuition, no grades, no power
grid: why are people flocking to a 'college' in the middle of the desert?" — which
became the most-read article on the paper's site the day it ran.

## 2027 — Season 8 (upcoming)

Mars 2027 is now recruiting, with rolling admissions, four ways to participate
(DPW build crew, Tool Camp, the Mars Institute of Technology AI program, and
Co Lab), build beginning November 2, 2026, the season proper starting January 4,
2027, and Mars Electronica on March 26–28, 2027. Details:
https://mars.college/mars-2027.md

## What changed, what stayed

Across seven seasons the cohort grew from ~20 to a multi-camp federation; governance
went from two founders to an elected council and circles; the treasury went from an
ad-hoc grocery pot to a transparent community fund; and the AI thread went from a
Raspberry Pi cluster to cloud GPUs, house bots, a declared major, and working
agents. What stayed: Bombay Beach, the pallet rack as the unit of architecture, the
winter season rhythm, the no-tuition gift-plus-treasury economy, the principle that
the village should demonstrate what it studies, and the identity "we are all
Martians."
